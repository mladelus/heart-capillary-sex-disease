# Analysis plan: do female and male heart capillaries differ in disease?

**Author:** Maria Adelus · **Draft, to be time-stamped on GitHub before any expression data are analyzed.** Later changes are listed under *Deviations*.

## Background and question

In healthy adult human hearts, capillary-function programs did not differ substantially between females and males (Adelus, paper 1). Sex differences in coronary microvascular disease may therefore arise in disease rather than at baseline.

**Primary question:** in diseased human ventricles, do female and male capillary endothelial cells differ in capillary-function programs?

**Secondary question:** is any sex difference in disease larger than in health (sex × disease)?

## Sex bias of the available diseases, and expectations (fixed before analysis)

All four diseases available in open single-cell data are more common or more severe in males. The female-predominant microvascular conditions (coronary microvascular dysfunction, HFpEF, INOCA, takotsubo) have no open single-nucleus heart data. This shapes the hypotheses; citations are to be verified before the manuscript is written.

| Disease | Sex pattern (clinical literature) | Expectation for capillaries in females vs males |
|---|---|---|
| DCM | More common in males; females often have better outcomes | Females less affected: a smaller capillary disease response |
| ACM | Males more often and more severely affected | Same as DCM (descriptive only; 3 females) |
| Myocarditis | Strong male predominance, especially in young adults | Females show less inflammatory/interferon activation in capillaries (descriptive only; 3 females) |
| MI | More common in males; females have worse microvascular outcomes (more microvascular obstruction and MINOCA) | Females show more capillary dysfunction (descriptive only; 4 females) |

**Directional secondary hypothesis (DCM, the only well-powered stratum):** in the same-consortium sex × disease model, the DCM-related change in capillary programs is smaller in females than in males. This is tested as the sex × disease interaction, two-sided, and reported with its direction.

## Data (all open; CELLxGENE Census release 2025-11-08)

- **Donors:** adults (18 years or older) with recorded sex and a non-normal disease label.
- **Tissue:** ventricular tissue only (same labels as paper 1).
- **Minimum cells:** at least 100 endothelial cells per donor. Only primary data are used.
- **Analysis strata:** one stratum per dataset × disease with at least 3 eligible females and 3 eligible males. The metadata inventory (run before this plan) found four:

| Stratum | Source | Females | Males |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) | Reichart et al. 2022 | 12 | 34 |
| Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (ACM) | Reichart et al. 2022 | 3 | 5 |
| Myocardial infarction (MI) | Kuppe et al. 2022 | 4 | 12 |
| Myocarditis | Census myocarditis dataset | 3 | 20 |
| **Total** | | **22** | **71** |

- **Healthy comparison:** the 45 healthy donors of paper 1 (23 females, 22 males), plus any healthy donors in the disease datasets.

## Methods (identical to paper 1 unless stated)

- **Cell definitions:** same broad cell classes; the same capillary, arterial and venous marker rule, applied within each dataset; pseudobulk minimums of at least 30 cells.
- **Outcome programs:** the six paper 1 programs (NO/eNOS, endothelin/ACE, prostacyclin, barrier/junction, fatty-acid transport, angiogenic tip), with the same genes. Scores are mean z-scores, standardized within each stratum.
- **Positive control:** the X-escape program.

## Primary analysis

- **Model:** within each stratum, score ~ sex + age (+ assay when it varies). The female-minus-male estimates are pooled across strata by random-effects meta-analysis (REML), since 4 strata are available.
- **Multiplicity:** Holm correction across the scorable programs.
- **Equivalence:** bounds of ±0.8 SD, as in paper 1. Whether the 90% CI also lies within ±0.5 SD is reported.
- **Verdicts:** *difference*, *equivalent* or *inconclusive*, by the paper 1 rules.
- **Calibration:** 1,000 sex-label permutations within stratum × assay.
- **Sensitivity analyses:**
  - adding the stress score and the ambient heart-muscle RNA score as covariates;
  - DCM alone;
  - leaving each stratum out in turn.

## Secondary analyses

1. **Sex × disease, same consortium.** This uses Reichart DCM together with the Heart Cell Atlas healthy donors (Litviňuková 2020), which come from the same consortium and snRNA-seq protocol.
   - **Model:** score ~ sex × disease + age + assay.
   - **Estimand:** the interaction, i.e., the change in the female-minus-male difference from health to DCM.
   - This analysis is reported as supportive, because disease and study are partly confounded.
2. **Sex × disease, all data.** The pooled disease estimate (primary) is compared with the pooled healthy estimate from paper 1 by a z-test of the difference. It is labeled cross-study.
3. **Pericytes:** the pericyte-to-capillary ratio and the five paper 1 pericyte programs, female minus male within disease.
4. **X–Y dosage:** KDM6A/UTY and KDM5C/KDM5D (X alone and X + Y) in capillary endothelium, female minus male within disease, compared with health.
5. **Disease positive control:** DCM vs healthy capillaries (same-consortium model). It must show clear disease effects, such as a significant shift in at least one program or many genes at FDR < 0.05. This shows that the pipeline detects biology in these data.

## What counts as a result

- **Positive:** the pooled Holm p < 0.05 with the same direction in most strata, and calibration passes. If the secondary sex × disease interaction also has p < 0.05, this is described as "sex differences emerge in disease".
- **Informative null:** equivalence (90% CI within ±0.8 SD).
- **Inconclusive:** anything else.

## Known limitations (stated in advance)

- Few females: 22 in total, 12 with DCM.
- All available diseases are male-biased, so results cannot be generalized to the female-predominant microvascular diseases, for which no open single-nucleus data exist.
- Disease severity, genetic cause and treatment vary and are not recorded.
- Health vs disease comparisons partly cross studies.
- Ages are partly recorded by decade.
- Menopause and hormone data are not available.

## Deviations

1. **Mural cells instead of pericytes (made before any pericyte or sex result was seen).** After download, the cell labels showed that two of the three datasets (DCM/ACM atlas; myocarditis) label pericytes and smooth muscle cells together as "mural cell", and only the myocardial infarction dataset labels pericytes separately. Using the "pericyte" label alone would have given most donors zero pericytes. For aim 3 we therefore use one rule for every dataset: mural cells = pericyte + mural cell + smooth muscle labels (cardiac mural cells are mostly pericytes). These cells are downloaded by a new script, `02b_download_mural.R`. The ratio becomes mural cells per capillary EC, and the five pericyte programs are scored in the mural-cell pseudobulk. The primary analysis (aims 1 and 2) is unchanged.

2. **Bug fix (no change to any estimate):** in script 04 the leave-one-stratum-out rows were labeled with the number of strata ("without 3") instead of the stratum left out, because the loop variable shared its name with a column. The loop variable was renamed.


## Stage 3 — where do sex differences lie? (added after the stage 1 and stage 2 results; committed before any stage 3 analysis was run)

Stages 1 (healthy, paper 1) and 2 (disease) found no large sex differences in the six pre-chosen capillary programs, but a reproducible X–Y dosage pattern. Stage 3 asks where sex differences in the heart are, without restricting to pre-chosen programs. Everything below is exploratory; the healthy cohort (45 donors) is the discovery set and the disease cohort (93 donors) the replication set, and vice versa. No new data are downloaded.

**A. Discovery and replication by cell type (script 08).** Cell types: capillary, arterial and venous endothelium; mural cells (pericyte label in stage 1, mural label in stage 2); fibroblasts; cardiomyocytes; myeloid and lymphoid cells. In each cohort and cell type, donor pseudobulks are analysed with limma-voom (expression ~ sex + age [+ assay]) within each dataset (stage 1) or disease (stage 2), and combined by inverse-variance meta-analysis. Genes are classed as autosomal, X or Y (EnsDb.Hsapiens.v86). Measures of autosomal sex signal per cell type, reported side by side: (i) the Spearman correlation of gene-level sex z-scores between the two cohorts; (ii) the number of autosomal genes at FDR < 0.05 in one cohort that replicate (p < 0.05, same direction) in the other, with 2.5% expected by chance. Pathways: Hallmark gene sets (MSigDB) tested on the combined (Stouffer) autosomal z-scores with a rank-based gene-set test, Benjamini–Hochberg across sets, per cell type. Expectation stated in advance: the autosomal sex signal in capillary endothelium is no larger than in other heart cell types.

**B. Sex-hormone receptor map (script 09).** ESR1, ESR2, GPER1, AR, PGR and CYP19A1: expression (log2 CPM) by cell type and sex in both cohorts; in capillaries, female − male by age band (< 50, ≥ 50 years); and Hallmark estrogen-response (early, late) and androgen-response scores in capillaries, female − male, combined across both cohorts.

**C. Capillary cell states (script 10).** Every capillary EC is scored for stress, interferon response, inflammatory activation, angiogenic tip and proliferation (mean log(1 + CP10k) of fixed gene lists). A cell is "high" for a state if its score is in the top 10% of capillary cells of its dataset; the outcome is each donor's logit fraction of high cells. The capillary-to-arterial/venous position of each capillary cell (z_venous − z_arterial, from script 03) is summarized per donor as its mean. Female − male with the primary methods (per dataset or disease, random-effects meta-analysis), in both cohorts and combined.

**D. Donor-to-donor variability (script 10).** For each program and the X-escape control, log(SD in females / SD in males) of the age-adjusted score within each dataset or disease, combined by meta-analysis; and the mean transcriptome distance (1 − Pearson correlation, 2,000 most variable autosomal genes) between donors of the same sex, females vs males, tested by sex-label permutation within dataset.

**Combined health + disease summaries (script 11).** Female − male estimates of the same outcome from both cohorts are combined by random-effects meta-analysis across all six strata (two healthy datasets, four disease strata), with disease state as a moderator. Figures for stage 3.

**E. Immune cells (script 12; added after stage 3 A–D results, committed before this analysis was run).** Stage 3 showed higher estrogen-response gene expression in females in myeloid and lymphoid cells and fibroblasts, the cell types with the highest ESR1 expression, but no gene-level sex differences replicated in immune cells. Question: do immune cells differ by sex in pre-chosen immune programs? Same methods as the primary analysis (donor pseudobulk, score standardized within dataset or disease, female − male adjusted for age and assay, random-effects meta-analysis per cohort and across all six strata, Holm across programs within a cell type, X-escape positive control).
Myeloid programs: type I interferon response (IFIT1, IFIT3, ISG15, MX1, IFI6, IFI44L, OAS1, STAT1, IRF7, XAF1); MHC class II antigen presentation (HLA-DRA, HLA-DRB1, HLA-DPA1, HLA-DPB1, HLA-DQA1, CD74, CIITA); inflammatory (IL1B, TNF, NLRP3, CCL3, CCL4, CXCL8, NFKBIA, PTGS2); resident macrophage (LYVE1, F13A1, MRC1, CD163, FOLR2, STAB1); recruited monocyte-derived (CCR2, PLAC8, S100A8, S100A9, VCAN, FCN1); complement/efferocytosis (C1QA, C1QB, C1QC, MERTK, TREM2, APOE).
Lymphoid programs: cytotoxic (GZMB, GZMK, GZMA, PRF1, NKG7, GNLY, CCL5); type I interferon response (as above); B cell (MS4A1, CD79A, CD79B, BANK1).
Also: (i) immune composition per donor (myeloid and lymphoid share of all cells, logit); (ii) immune X-linked genes reported to escape X inactivation (TLR7, TLR8, CXCR3, CD40LG, BTK, IL2RG, CYBB), female − male from script 08; (iii) donor-level Hallmark estrogen-response (early) and oxidative-phosphorylation scores in every cell type, female − male, with and without a heart-muscle ambient RNA score as covariate, to test whether the estrogen signal tracks ESR1 expression and whether the male-higher oxidative-phosphorylation signal is contamination. Limitation known in advance: the myocardial infarction dataset labels many lymphoid cells with terms outside our lymphoid class, so lymphoid results there are incomplete.

**F. Cardiomyocyte sex-biased genes: pathways and network (script 13; exploratory, added after stage 3 A–E, committed before this analysis was run).** Stage 3 A found replicated autosomal sex differences only in cardiomyocytes. Gene lists: (i) "replicated" = FDR < 0.05 in one cohort and p < 0.05 with the same direction in the other; (ii) "consistent" = p < 0.05 with the same direction in both cohorts and combined (Stouffer) FDR < 0.05; each also split by direction. Pathways: over-representation (hypergeometric) against the background of all autosomal genes tested in cardiomyocytes, for Hallmark, Reactome and GO biological process sets of 10–500 genes, Benjamini–Hochberg within collection; and a competitive rank-based test that allows for inter-gene correlation (limma cameraPR, inter-gene correlation 0.01) on the combined z-scores of all tested autosomal genes. Network: STRING protein–protein interactions (confidence ≥ 0.4) among the listed genes, the STRING test of whether they have more interactions than expected, and the same network with up to 10 added partner proteins. The pathway test used in script 08 (which assumed independent genes) is superseded by these. Also reported: gene-level female − male estimates for the endothelin/ACE program genes in capillary endothelium (EDN1, ECE1, EDNRB, ACE, plus EDNRA and ECE2 for context).

(A) EDN1 and EDNRB, which were higher in female capillary endothelium in both single-cell cohorts: female − male in bulk tissue (limma-voom; age band, RNA integrity, ischemic time, Hardy class), with and without adjustment for a capillary-content score, and by age band (20–49, 50–79 years). Prediction: EDN1 is higher in females. (B) The cardiomyocyte sex-biased genes of script 13: proportion with the same direction in GTEx (sign test against 50%) and with the same direction and p < 0.05, compared with the background rate among all autosomal genes; and the genome-wide correlation between single-cell cardiomyocyte and GTEx sex effects. **H. Human genetics of the X-linked paralogs (script 16; exploratory, committed before this analysis was run).** Stages 1–3 found that females have lower combined X + Y expression of KDM6A/UTY, KDM5C/KDM5D and USP9X/USP9Y. Question: is there human genetic evidence that these loci matter for cardiovascular traits? Step 1 (lookup): all associations in the NHGRI-EBI GWAS Catalog (GRCh38) within 500 kb of the eight X-linked genes of our pairs (KDM6A, KDM5C, USP9X, DDX3X, EIF1AX, ZFX, RPS4X, NLGN4X), classified by keyword as cardiovascular, cardiometabolic, sex-hormone or reproductive, blood or immune, or other; and a comparison of the share of cardiovascular and cardiometabolic associations inside these windows with the rest of the X chromosome (Fisher's exact test). Step 2 (expression): significant single-tissue eQTLs for the same genes in GTEx v8 heart, artery and blood, and whether any eQTL variant is itself a catalogued GWAS variant. Prediction stated in advance: variants near KDM6A or KDM5C are associated with cardiovascular or cardiometabolic traits. Known limits: the X chromosome is omitted from many GWAS, so absence of an association is weak evidence; a variant near a gene need not act through it; no catalogued trait is specific to the coronary microcirculation; Y-linked variation cannot be examined with open data. A third step, comparing female and male effect sizes in sex-stratified summary statistics, will be planned only if steps 1–2 identify candidate variants.

*Significance criteria for H, fixed before running.* Because eight regions chosen in advance are examined rather than the whole genome, two tiers are reported and always labeled: "genome-wide" (p < 5 × 10⁻⁸) and "suggestive" (5 × 10⁻⁸ ≤ p < 1 × 10⁻⁵, the GWAS Catalog's inclusion threshold). Suggestive associations are treated as leads, not findings. Windows are 500 kb (primary) and 1 Mb (sensitivity). The enrichment test uses p < 0.05. In the candidate eQTL lookup, each catalogued cardiovascular or cardiometabolic variant near a gene is tested as an eQTL for that gene in left ventricle, coronary artery and aorta; significance is Bonferroni-corrected across the tests actually made, and nominal p < 0.05 is reported as suggestive. These criteria will not be changed after the results are seen.

**I. Wider genetic look (script 17; exploratory; added after the results of H were seen, committed before this analysis was run).** H looked only near eight X-linked genes and found no enrichment. Because that search was narrow, the same catalogue is now examined for wider, clearly labeled gene sets. This widening was decided after seeing H, so everything here is hypothesis-generating. Gene sets: (1) the cardiomyocyte sex-biased genes (55 consistent; 31 replicated); (2) X-linked genes higher in females in both single-cell cohorts in any cell type (our own escape-gene list); (3) eighteen X-linked members of X–Y gene pairs; (4) the genes of the six capillary programs; (5) endothelin genes (EDN1, EDNRA, EDNRB, ECE1) and the PHACTR1 locus that regulates EDN1. For each set: the proportion of genes that the catalogue maps to at least one genome-wide significant (p < 5 × 10⁻⁸) cardiovascular association, compared with a background of other genes expressed in the same cell type (or other X-linked genes), by Fisher's exact test; the same for cardiometabolic traits and for any trait (a control for gene size and annotation density). Prediction: the cardiomyocyte sex-biased genes are mapped to cardiovascular associations more often than other cardiomyocyte-expressed genes. Separately, all catalogued associations at the EDN1/PHACTR1 locus are listed, noting conditions that mainly affect women (migraine, fibromuscular dysplasia, arterial dissection, microvascular angina). Limits: mapped genes are nearest genes, not proven causal genes; long genes collect more associations; no correction is made across the five gene sets, so a single nominal result is a lead only.

*Addition to I, made before script 17 was run with it:* the catalogue's mapped gene is only the nearest gene, so a distance-based version is added. Genome-wide cardiovascular associations are counted within 100 kb, 500 kb and 1 Mb of every gene, and each gene set is compared with its background at each window size (Fisher's exact test for any association; Wilcoxon test for the count). Windows wider than 1 Mb are not used, because cis-regulation beyond 1 Mb is uncommon and each wider window would contain dozens of unrelated genes. Script 16 additionally lists the associations lying between 500 kb and 1 Mb from each X-linked gene.

**J. **K. Which capillary cells make endothelin-1, and the endothelin variant in GTEx (script 19; exploratory, committed before this analysis was run).**
(1) *Single-cell characterization.* Within each donor, capillary endothelial cells with at least one EDN1 transcript ("EDN1-positive") are compared with EDN1-negative capillary cells from the same donor that are matched on sequencing depth (same within-donor depth decile, up to three negatives per positive), because detecting a sparse gene depends on depth. Donors with fewer than five positive cells are excluded. Analyses, all with the donor as the unit: (i) paired differential expression of positive vs matched-negative pseudobulks (limma-voom, donor as a blocking factor), genes at FDR < 0.05; (ii) per-donor differences in state scores (stress, interferon, inflammatory activation, angiogenic tip, hypoxia, shear-stress/KLF2), position on the arterial–venous axis, and the share of cells expressing EDNRB, tested across donors with a one-sample t test; (iii) among positive cells, EDN1 transcripts per cell (depth-normalized) in females vs males, to separate "more cells" from "more per cell". Cells are never treated as independent observations. (2) *GTEx look-ups.* The reported EDN1-regulating variant rs9349379: its association with EDN1 and PHACTR1 expression in heart and artery (GTEx v8 calculator), and whether EDN1, PHACTR1 or the other genes highlighted in this study appear in the GTEx list of sex-biased eQTLs, if that open file can be downloaded. Limits: EDN1-positive cells are few per donor, so (i) has little power for modest differences; positivity partly reflects depth even after matching; none of this tests whether the sex difference is real.

**L. A map of the endothelial cells (script 20; descriptive only, added before this script was run).** A two-dimensional UMAP of the blood endothelial cells of all 138 donors, for the figures. Up to 300 cells per donor are drawn at random (fixed seed) so that no donor dominates. The 2,000 most variable autosomal genes are used (X-linked, Y-linked and mitochondrial genes are left out, so that sex is not built into the map), followed by 30 principal components, alignment of the five datasets with Harmony, UMAP and graph clustering (resolution 0.4). Cells are coloured by the subtype assigned in script 03, sex, dataset or disease, and EDN1. No statistical test is based on the map or on the clusters, and cells are never treated as independent observations; every inference in the paper remains at the donor level. Figures for the combined manuscript are drawn from the saved result files with the scripts in `figures/` (Python, matplotlib); they display results and compute no new statistics.

**M. Is there an endothelial state, of which EDN1 is one marker, that is more abundant in females? (scripts in `state/`, Python; exploratory; written on 2 October 2026 before any of these analyses was run).** Sections J and K found a larger share of EDN1-expressing capillary cells in females and a distinct profile of those cells. Because EDN1 was chosen after seeing a sex difference, a state defined by EDN1 itself is open to the objection that the gene that produced the hypothesis also defines the state. The analyses below ask whether the result holds for a state defined without EDN1. They use the cell-level counts already downloaded; no new data are downloaded except where stated. The donor is the unit of every test.

*M1. Signature, learned without reference to sex.* In each donor with at least five EDN1-positive capillary cells, the positive cells are compared with EDN1-negative capillary cells of the same donor matched on sequencing depth (as in K; fixed seed). For each gene with at least 10 counts in the two groups together in at least half of the donors, the per-donor difference in log2 counts per million (0.5 added) is tested across donors with a one-sample t test (Benjamini–Hochberg). The "up" signature is the genes with FDR < 0.05 and a positive mean difference; the "down" signature those with a negative mean difference. Removed from both, so that nothing is built in: all endothelin genes (EDN1, EDN2, EDN3, ECE1, ECE2, EDNRA, EDNRB), the twelve markers used to assign vessel type (CA4, RGCC, BTNL9, GJA5, HEY1, SEMA3G, GJA4, DKK2, FBLN5, ACKR1, NR2F2, CPE), the flow-response genes (KLF2, KLF4, NOS3, THBD) and X- and Y-linked genes. *Cross-fitting:* to avoid scoring cells with a signature learned from the same cells, the signature learned in the healthy cohort is applied to the diseased cohort and the signature learned in the diseased cohort to the healthy cohort. An all-donor signature is used only as a sensitivity analysis and for the genetic analysis (M5).

*M2. State score and the primary test.* For every blood endothelial cell, state score = log2((counts of up genes + 1) / (counts of down genes + 1)), a within-cell ratio. A cell is "state-high" if its score is above the 90th percentile of the capillary cells of its dataset. Per donor (at least 30 capillary cells): **abundance** = logit of the fraction of capillary cells that are state-high; **intensity** = mean score of the state-high cells. Female − male within each dataset or disease (linear model with age, and assay where it varies; SD units), random-effects meta-analysis across the six strata, exactly as in the earlier stages. **Primary test of M: abundance, cross-fitted signature, all six strata.** Secondary: the same with median log UMI per cell as a covariate; intensity; the all-donor signature; thresholds at the 80th and 95th percentiles. Prediction: abundance is higher in females and intensity is not. *Validation that the signature marks the same cells without EDN1:* in each held-out donor, the area under the ROC curve of the score for EDN1-positive versus EDN1-negative capillary cells (mean across donors, compared with 0.5).

*M3. A continuum rather than a cluster.* Arterial position of a cell = z(arterial markers) − z(capillary markers) from script 03. For capillary- and arterial-assigned cells together, cells are placed in ten bins of arterial position within dataset; per donor and bin, the mean state score, the flow-response score (KLF2, KLF4, NOS3, THBD), and the share of EDN1-positive cells are computed and shown by sex. Within each donor, the Spearman correlations of the state score with arterial position and with the flow-response score are computed across capillary cells and tested against zero across donors. The distribution of the state score among capillary cells is examined for bimodality (descriptive; Hartigan's dip is not computed).

*M4. Capillary cells or misassigned arteriolar cells?* The abundance test of M2 is repeated in: (i) capillary cells by the original rule; (ii) strictly defined capillary cells, z(capillary) − max(z(arterial), z(venous)) ≥ 1; (iii) cells that the source atlas also labels as capillary endothelial cells, where the atlas provides such labels; (iv) thirds of arterial position within capillary-assigned cells; (v) arterial-assigned and venous-assigned cells. The threshold for "state-high" is the one defined in M2 and is not recomputed. Interpretation fixed in advance: if the female excess is present in (ii) and (iii), it is a property of capillary cells; if it is present only in the arterial third of (iv) or in (v), it concerns the arteriole–capillary transition; if it is absent everywhere except (i), the finding of J depends on the assignment rule.

*M5. Genetics matched to the state.* Using the GWAS Catalog file already downloaded: for the all-donor signature (up and down genes together, and up genes alone) versus the other genes tested in M1 (the rest of the capillary transcriptome), the odds of being a mapped gene of at least one genome-wide significant association (p < 5 × 10⁻⁸) for five trait groups chosen in advance for their link to the hypothesis: coronary artery disease or myocardial infarction; blood pressure or hypertension; migraine; arterial dissection or fibromuscular dysplasia; heart failure. Logistic regression with log gene length and mean capillary expression as covariates, because long and highly expressed genes collect more associations. Controls: "any trait", and two non-vascular trait groups (height; educational attainment). Five tests; Bonferroni threshold 0.01; nominal results are reported as leads. A gene-based test from full summary statistics (MAGMA) and a colocalization analysis of rs9349379 with EDN1 and PHACTR1 expression need downloads of summary statistics and reference panels and are not part of M; they are left as stated future work.

*Limits stated in advance.* The signature is learned from EDN1-positive cells, so "EDN1-independent" means that EDN1 and related genes are not in the score, not that the state was discovered without EDN1. With ten percent of cells called state-high by construction in each dataset, the test concerns differences between donors within a dataset. Cross-fitting reduces, but does not remove, dependence between learning and scoring, because both cohorts were used in K. All of M is hypothesis-generating.

*Addendum to M (post hoc, 2 October 2026, after M1–M4 were seen).* Because EDN1-expressing cells have a lower flow-response score, one further check was run: whether the flow-response program itself (KLF2, KLF4, NOS3, THBD) differs between female and male capillary cells (donor-level score from script 18, and the mean per-cell score), with the same model. **N. Replication of the endothelial state in an independent cohort (written on 2 October 2026, before the replication data were downloaded or opened).** Section M gave a female excess of state-high capillary cells of +0.36 SD (95% CI −0.03 to 0.75; p = 0.068) with a cross-fitted signature. That estimate comes from the same 138 donors in which the hypothesis arose. Section N tests it once, in donors that have not been used.

*Why not the remaining Census donors.* The 23 eligible healthy donors of the Census that are not in the 138 (six small datasets) include only six females, at most two per dataset; they cannot support a within-dataset comparison and are not used.

*Cohort.* Koenig et al. 2022 (Nat Cardiovasc Res 1:263–280; GEO GSE183852; open access): left ventricle of 45 individuals, 38 profiled by single-nucleus and 7 by single-cell RNA sequencing; non-diseased donors and dilated cardiomyopathy. None of these individuals is in the Census datasets used above. Only the public count matrix is used. *Strata:* disease group within technology (for example, donor nuclei; cardiomyopathy nuclei); a stratum enters with at least three females and three males who each have at least 30 capillary cells, as before.

*Sex.* GEO does not list sex. Sex is assigned per sample from the data, before any outcome is computed: female if XIST is expressed and Y-linked genes (RPS4Y1, DDX3Y, UTY, KDM5D, EIF1AY, USP9Y) are essentially absent, male if the reverse; samples that fit neither pattern are excluded. If the published sample table can be obtained, it takes precedence and disagreements are reported.

*Cells.* The public matrix carries no cell-type labels, so blood endothelial cells are identified by markers, with rules fixed here: for each cell, class scores are the mean log(1 + counts per 10,000) of endothelial (PECAM1, VWF, CDH5, EGFL7, EMCN, FLT1), cardiomyocyte (TTN, RYR2, MYH7, MYBPC3), fibroblast (DCN, COL1A2, PDGFRA, LUM), mural (RGS5, PDGFRB, ACTA2, MYH11, NOTCH3), myeloid (CD163, F13A1, C1QA, CSF1R), lymphoid (PTPRC, CD3E, CD247, NKG7), lymphatic or endocardial (PROX1, CCL21, NPR3) and neural or adipocyte (NRXN1, PLP1, ADIPOQ, PLIN1) markers, z-scored across all cells of the technology; a cell is a blood endothelial cell if the endothelial score is the highest and at least two endothelial markers are detected. Capillary, arterial and venous cells are then assigned with the marker rule of script 03, z-scored within technology.

*Signature and score.* The signature is frozen: the all-donor signature of section M (up and down genes as saved in `state/out/signatures.pkl` on 2 October 2026 and listed in Additional file 1, Table S26), with EDN1, the other endothelin genes, the vessel markers, the flow-response genes and X/Y genes already removed. The state score, the 90th-percentile threshold among capillary cells (within technology), abundance and intensity are computed exactly as in M2.

*Tests.* **Primary (one test): abundance of state-high capillary cells, female − male, within stratum (age as a covariate only if age is available), random-effects meta-analysis across strata; two-sided, α = 0.05; predicted direction, higher in females.** Secondary: share of EDN1-positive capillary cells; abundance in strictly defined capillary cells; intensity; abundance adjusted for median log UMI; validation of the frozen signature (area under the curve for EDN1-positive versus EDN1-negative capillary cells within depth deciles); the flow-response score of EDN1-positive versus depth-matched EDN1-negative cells. A combined estimate across the six original strata and the new strata is reported as secondary.

*Interpretation, fixed in advance.* "Replicated": primary estimate positive with p < 0.05. "Consistent but not conclusive": estimate positive, p ≥ 0.05, and 95% CI includes +0.36. "Not replicated": estimate zero or negative, or 95% CI excludes +0.36. Whatever the outcome, it is reported in the manuscript.

*Limits.* Marker-based identification of endothelial cells differs from the atlas labels used in the Census cohorts; sex is inferred; the number of females is unknown until the data are opened and may be too small for a stratum to enter, in which case the replication is reported as not feasible in this cohort.

**O. Gene-set test of the endothelial state against full GWAS summary statistics (written on 4 October 2026, before any summary-statistics file was downloaded or opened).** M5 used only the list of mapped genes in the GWAS Catalog, which ignores all sub-threshold signal and depends on how each study reported its loci. Section O repeats the question with a standard gene-set method on full summary statistics. It tests whether the genes of the endothelial state carry more genetic association with vascular disease than other genes expressed in capillary endothelial cells. It does not test a sex difference.

*Gene set and comparison genes, frozen.* The all-donor signature of section M as saved on 2 October 2026 (Table S26). Gene universe: the 1,866 autosomal capillary-expressed genes with a genomic position used in M5. **Primary set: the 271 state genes in that universe (up and down together).** Secondary sets: the 164 up genes; the 107 down genes. EDN1 and the other endothelin genes are not in the signature by construction.

*Traits, fixed in advance.* Primary (four): coronary artery disease (Aragam et al. 2022, European-ancestry meta-analysis); spontaneous coronary artery dissection (Adlam et al. 2023); heart failure (Shah et al. 2020, HERMES); systolic blood pressure (Evangelou et al. 2018). Negative controls (two): height (Yengo et al. 2018) and educational attainment (Lee et al. 2018). Migraine and fibromuscular dysplasia were in M5 but are left out here because their complete summary statistics are not openly downloadable.

*Eligibility rule.* A file is used only if it can be downloaded without registration or an application. Step 0, done before any analysis, records for each trait whether the file was obtained, its source, genome build, sample size and number of variants. If a planned file is not openly available, the trait is dropped and this is recorded; it is not replaced by another trait after results are seen. If the European-ancestry file of a study is not available but its multi-ancestry file is, the multi-ancestry file is used with the European reference panel and this is stated as a limitation.

*Method.* MAGMA (de Leeuw et al. 2015), current version. Variants assigned to genes on GRCh37 with a window of 35 kb upstream and 10 kb downstream; gene locations from the MAGMA NCBI37.3 file, matched to the signature by gene symbol; linkage disequilibrium from the 1000 Genomes phase 3 European panel supplied with MAGMA; SNP-wise mean model; per-variant sample size where the file gives it, otherwise the study total. The extended MHC region (chromosome 6, 25–34 Mb) is excluded. Competitive gene-set analysis restricted to the gene universe (so the comparison is with other capillary-expressed genes and not with the whole genome), with MAGMA's default adjustment for gene size, variant density and their logs, and additionally conditioned on mean capillary expression (log2 counts per million, as in M5). The test is one-sided (more association in the set), as MAGMA reports it.

*Tests and thresholds.* **Primary: the 271-gene set in each of the four primary traits; Bonferroni α = 0.05 / 4 = 0.0125.** Secondary, reported without a claim: up and down sets separately; the same tests without the expression covariate; the same tests against all autosomal genes; the primary test after removing genes within 1 Mb of rs9349379 (PHACTR1/EDN1 locus), to show whether one locus carries the result.

*Interpretation, fixed in advance.* "Supported": at least one primary trait with p < 0.0125 and both negative controls with p > 0.05. "Not specific": a primary trait passes but a negative control has p < 0.05. "Not supported": no primary trait passes. All six traits are reported in the manuscript whatever the outcome.

*Limits, stated in advance.* The signature was learned in the same hearts in which the hypothesis arose, but genetic association is independent of that; gene-set tests with a 271-gene set in a 1,866-gene universe have modest power; window-based assignment of variants to genes misses distal regulation; the result concerns the cell state, not sex. Sex-stratified summary statistics will be used only if an open file is found for a primary trait, as an exploratory analysis labelled as such. Colocalization at rs9349379 remains not done.

*Step 0 of O as recorded (4 October 2026, before any gene-level analysis).* Only the GWAS Catalog study records, the file listings, the column headers and the first two rows of each file were read, to learn the format. Files, all from the open GWAS Catalog summary-statistics archive: coronary artery disease, GCST90132314 (European ancestry; 181,522 cases, 984,168 controls; harmonised file, variants matched by rsID); spontaneous coronary artery dissection, GCST90245878 (1,917 cases, 9,292 controls); heart failure, GCST009541 (47,309 cases, 930,014 controls); systolic blood pressure, GCST006624 (757,601 individuals; variants named by GRCh37 position and matched to rsIDs through the reference panel); height, GCST006901. Educational attainment (Lee et al. 2018) has no openly downloadable file in the archive and is dropped under the eligibility rule, so height is the only negative control and the criterion "both negative controls" applies to height alone. MAGMA v1.10, gene locations build 37 and the European 1000 Genomes panel are downloaded from the MAGMA site. The analysis against all autosomal genes cannot be conditioned on capillary expression, which is known only for the gene universe. Script: `genetics/O1_magma.R`; gene list: `genetics/state_genes.csv`. **P. The endothelin-1-producing, low-flow-response endothelial state in the human kidney (written on 4 October 2026, before any kidney expression data were downloaded or opened for this question).** Section N reproduced the cell state in an independent heart cohort (the frozen signature recognised EDN1-positive capillary cells, and those cells again had a lower flow-response score). Section P asks whether the same state exists in the endothelium of a second organ, the kidney, and, as a secondary question, whether its abundance differs by sex there.

*Data.* Adult human kidney in the CELLxGENE Census (release 2025-11-08), from the Kidney Precision Medicine Project atlas (Lake et al. 2023) and any other kidney dataset with donor sex. Step 0 (script `kidney/K0_inventory.R`) reads cell labels only (no expression) and fixes, before expression is read: the datasets used; the strata (dataset and assay × disease group: healthy reference, chronic kidney disease, acute kidney injury), each requiring at least three females and three males with at least 100 blood endothelial cells; and which endothelial labels exist. A donor present in more than one dataset is used once, in the dataset with more endothelial cells.

*Cell populations.* Blood endothelial cells by the atlas labels (lymphatic excluded). If the labels separate endothelial types, the **primary population is peritubular capillary endothelium**, the closest counterpart of myocardial capillaries; glomerular capillary endothelium, arteriolar endothelium and vasa recta are analysed separately as secondary populations. If the labels do not separate them, the primary population is all blood endothelial cells and this is recorded in step 0. Donors need at least 30 cells of a population to enter its analysis.

*Signature and score.* The frozen all-donor heart signature of section M (Table S26; no endothelin gene, no flow-response gene, no X or Y gene), the state score, the 90th-percentile threshold (within stratum technology, among cells of the primary population), abundance and intensity, all exactly as in N. Nothing is re-learned in kidney.

*Primary tests (two; Bonferroni α = 0.025 each).* Within donors with enough EDN1-positive cells (same minimum as N), in the primary population: (1) the area under the curve of the state score for EDN1-positive versus EDN1-negative cells within sequencing-depth deciles, mean across donors against 0.5; (2) the flow-response score (KLF2, KLF4, NOS3, THBD) of EDN1-positive cells minus that of depth-matched EDN1-negative cells of the same donor, mean across donors against 0. Both by one-sample t-test, two-sided; predicted directions, above 0.5 and below 0.

*Interpretation, fixed in advance.* "State present in kidney": both primary tests pass in the predicted direction. "Signature recognised, flow profile not shared": test 1 passes, test 2 does not. "Not found in kidney": test 1 does not pass. Whatever the outcome, it is reported.

*Secondary.* The same two tests in each secondary population and in each stratum; share of EDN1-positive cells by endothelial type. Sex: abundance of state-high cells and share of EDN1-positive cells, female − male, within stratum, adjusted for age where available, random-effects meta-analysis across strata, judged against the original +0.36 SD by the three categories of section N; sex × chronic kidney disease interaction where a dataset holds both groups. These are labelled exploratory: the female excess was not reproduced in N.

*Control specific to kidney.* Tubular cells express EDN1, and ambient RNA from tubules could make an endothelial nucleus appear EDN1-positive. Therefore: an ambient score per cell (LRP2, CUBN, SLC34A1, UMOD, as in the earlier kidney work) is compared between EDN1-positive and EDN1-negative cells, and both primary tests are repeated after excluding cells with any count of these four genes. If the primary tests pass only before this exclusion, the result is reported as not separable from ambient RNA.

*Limits, stated in advance.* The signature was learned in heart, so absence in kidney would not question the heart result; kidney endothelial types differ from myocardial capillaries in flow and structure; biopsies and nephrectomy tissue differ in handling; age and clinical detail in the public data are coarse.

**Q. Sex differences in the human kidney beyond the endothelial state (written on 4 October 2026, before any kidney expression data were downloaded for these questions).** An earlier analysis by the author of the Kidney Precision Medicine Project single-nucleus atlas (not part of this repository) found a modest sex × chronic kidney disease (CKD) interaction in interferon-γ signaling of glomerular capillary endothelium (self-contained test p = 0.007; not visible in a per-donor average score), X–Y dosage differences in kidney endothelium, a low rate of loss of Y, and higher ESR1 in glomerular endothelium of women that weakened in a second dataset. Women minus men throughout; age as a covariate where available.

*Q1. Replication of the sex × CKD interferon-γ interaction (the only confirmatory test of section Q).* Donors: adults with healthy reference kidney or CKD who were not in the single-nucleus cohort of the earlier analysis (the single-cell dataset of the same atlas and any other kidney dataset in the Census that holds both groups with at least three females and three males in each). **R. What regulates the cardiomyocyte sex-biased genes? (written on 5 October 2026, before any of these analyses was run).** The 55 consistent cardiomyocyte genes of stage 3 are not enriched for any functional pathway (script 13). Genes shifted by sex need not share a function; they may share a regulator. Section R asks whether the sex bias of cardiomyocyte genes is tied to sex-hormone receptors or to the X–Y regulators whose dosage differs by sex (sections F and H). Script `R/21_cm_regulators.R`. Exploratory throughout.

*R1. Targets of regulators.* Target-gene libraries from chromatin-binding experiments: MSigDB GTRD transcription-factor targets (primary library), and Enrichr ChEA 2022 and ENCODE (human experiments only; secondary). Three classes fixed in advance: hormone receptors (AR, ESR1, ESR2, PGR); X–Y demethylases (KDM5C, KDM5D, KDM6A, UTY); ZFX/ZFY. For each class, the union of its target sets among the autosomal genes tested in cardiomyocytes. **Primary: whether targets of a class are more sex-biased than other genes (competitive test on the absolute combined z-score, allowing for inter-gene correlation), one-sided, Bonferroni α = 0.05 / 3; to count as specific, the class must also lie in the top 10% of all factors in the library by mean absolute z-score of their targets.** Secondary: each regulator singly; direction (signed z-score); odds of the 55 genes among targets, adjusted for expression level; the related, non-sex-linked KDM5A, KDM5B and KDM6B for comparison. A class with no target set in a library is reported as not testable.

*R2. Dose within each sex.* A female-pattern score per donor from cardiomyocyte pseudobulks: mean z-score of the 15 female-higher genes minus that of the 40 male-higher genes, within stratum. Within each sex separately and within stratum, the slope of this score on the cardiomyocyte expression of AR, ESR1 and PGR, and on the combined X + Y dose of KDM5C/KDM5D, KDM6A/UTY and ZFX/ZFY, adjusted for age (and assay); pooled across strata. Because any two expression measures from the same nuclei correlate for technical reasons, the p value is empirical: the same slope for 300 random pairs of gene sets of the same sizes. Holm correction across the twelve tests.

*R3. Age.* In GTEx left ventricle (432 donors), the female-minus-male difference of the same score before and after age 50, with the covariates of script 14; the sex × age-band interaction is tested against 300 random gene sets of the same sizes. A difference that is larger before 50 would point to ovarian hormones; a difference of the same size in both bands would point to a stable origin (sex chromosomes or early-life programming).

*Interpretation, fixed in advance.* A regulator class is called "supported" only if R1 passes for it and R2 or R3 agrees in direction. If nothing passes, the regulator of these genes is reported as not identified from expression data.

*Limits.* Binding experiments come from cell lines, mostly not cardiomyocytes; target sets for KDM5D and UTY may not exist; expression of a receptor is a weak proxy for hormone exposure; R2 and R3 are associations.
